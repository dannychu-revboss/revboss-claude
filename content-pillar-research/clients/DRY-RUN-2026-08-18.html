The point of a dry run

Two bugs, both caught on real data

Both would have produced confident, wrong output on live clients. Both are fixed and committed.

The coverage read was blind to whole statuses

A default ordinal_search_content page returned only ToDo and Posted items for JustFund and David Freeman — hiding every Scheduled post. On that data the agent reported David's flagship pillar as starved when he in fact has three posts on it going out between 8/19 and 8/24. A false starvation flag sends the team chasing content a client already has, which is worse than no flag.

Fixed: the spec now requires enumerating every status explicitly, filtering per profile on multi-seat workspaces, and confirming any starvation flag against a full pull before reporting it.

Planning ahead read as starvation

Coverage only counted queued posts landing inside a 21-day window. JustFund plans to mid-November, so all eleven of her queued posts fell outside it and every pillar came back starved.

Fixed: three buckets now — published, queued near, queued further out. Starvation means zero in both forward buckets. Coverage that exists but lands past the window is far-out, a scheduling problem rather than a content one.

And the validator earned its keep

It rejected match terms short enough to mis-tag: ai matches "training", pri matches "priority", atm matches "statement". Left alone, those inflate coverage and hide exactly the starvation this system exists to catch.

Clients three, four, five

AGP is one account with three voices

Whitney, Alyssa and Trent share a market, a research pass, and — right now — some of the same arguments. Handling them as three unrelated clients is what produced the collisions below; handling them as one organization with assigned territory is the fix, and it also means the market research runs once instead of three times.

The collisions

  • conflictSame thesis, same source, three weeks apart. Whitney's 8/25 post ("giving is down is the wrong headline — generosity relocated") and Trent's 9/22 post ("generosity didn't shrink, it scattered") are the same argument off the same Fundraising Effectiveness Project data. One has to change. The proposal assigns sector-level giving data to Trent as CEO, and gives Whitney the practitioner-level cut.
  • conflictThe same burnout statistic in one window. Trent cites CEP's State of Nonprofits on 9/8 and 9/10; Alyssa cites the same research on 9/22. The proposal splits the territory: nonprofit-CEO burnout is Trent's, the CDO seat is Alyssa's.
  • source riskEvery queued post across all three seats is CONVERGE-derived — their own May summit. It's excellent raw material and it is a finite well. When it runs dry there is no next source, which is precisely the problem this agent is meant to replace.
  • stale dataAll three are working from annual FEP figures. The Q1 2026 cut is out: dollars up 4.3%, donor counts down 0.8%, retention 18% against 18.2% in 2024, repeat donors now ~61% of all dollars and recaptured donors another 15%. CCS's 2026 Pulse adds that 68% of nonprofits name acquisition as a top challenge, 48% retention.
  • openingThat data creates a real post for Trent: his queued line is that only ~2% of lapsed donors ever return, while recaptured donors now contribute 15% of all dollars. Both are true. The gap between them is the argument — recapture is undervalued because the per-donor odds look bad.

What this dry run is not

  • Counts are directional, not audited. I pulled samples of 5–20 items per client, not every post. The coverage tables are good enough to reason about and not good enough to quote — a real run enumerates every status and pages to the end.
  • Almost every buyer question is mine, not a buyer's. One question across all five sets came from a client's own words. Invented questions produce invented findings, so the sets are marked and the phrasing should be replaced from call transcripts before any baseline is measured. There are three Whitney transcripts and Client Context Docs for all three AGP seats on file to mine.
  • No answer-surface reading exists yet. That is the step you own, and it is the reason no brief is attached to this report. Pillars can be proposed without it; a client brief cannot be finished without it.
  • Four of the five have no voice bundle. Only Iara has a calibrated writer skill. Pillars tell a writer what to say; they don't fix how it sounds.
  • Two titles are unconfirmed and one ICP is inferred. Whitney's and Alyssa's roles are placeholders reading "CONFIRM," and the AGP ICP was read off post content because no ICP framework doc exists for those seats. Neither guess belongs in front of a client.
